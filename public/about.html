About Monterey Sea Otters

Introduction

Monterey Bay is home to one of the most beloved sea otter populations in the world, attracting visitors from around the globe. The southern sea otters living here are a distinct subspecies found only along the central California coast. These playful creatures have become iconic symbols of Monterey Bay, floating on their backs among the kelp forests.

Life in Monterey Bay

The cold, nutrient-rich waters of Monterey Bay provide the perfect habitat for sea otters, offering abundant food like sea urchins, crabs, and abalone. Sea otters spend most of their day floating in the kelp forests, often wrapping themselves in kelp fronds to anchor in place while they rest. The Monterey Bay Aquarium has played a crucial role in rehabilitating injured and orphaned otters.

Conservation

Once hunted to near extinction for their dense fur, southern sea otters were thought to be gone until a small group of about 50 was discovered near Big Sur in 1938. Thanks to decades of protection efforts centered around Monterey Bay, the population has slowly grown to around 3,000 today. Local organizations continue to monitor and protect these otters for future generations.
